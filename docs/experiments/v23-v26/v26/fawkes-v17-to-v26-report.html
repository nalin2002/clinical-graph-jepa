▸ THE OBJECTIVE — and the one number that measures it
Objective. Show that a Graph-JEPA world model over per-admission clinical knowledge graphs can recover held-out clinical relationships — specifically the four LLM-inferred cross-links (MANAGED_FOR, INDICATES, COMPLICATED_BY, CONFIRMS) that carry the paper's claim.

The metric that IS the objective = leave-one-out (LOO) MRR. Mask exactly ONE edge, keep the entire rest of the graph as context, rank the true tail against same-type candidates in the same graph, filtered. No randomness — exactly reproducible given a model and a test set. It is what the paper reports and what every number in this log is measured on unless a row says batch-mask. LOO evaluates almost exclusively the four inferred relations: the filtered protocol drops patient-anchored backbone edges, because after removing the other true tails of (patient, relation) fewer than two candidates usually remain.

Highest observed mean on the objective (10 paired seeds, fixed split): v23 uniform-span injection — patch masking + MLP readout, LOO MRR 0.4778 ± 0.0204. It is numerically +0.0098 over the matched v23 global-mean control, but the paired 95% CI [−0.0128, +0.0324] crosses zero, so it is not an established improvement. The uncomfortable finding underneath it (v19 and v23): neither changing the JEPA pretext task on top of the note nor adding learned note attention has improved the primary endpoint; the readout head and encoder trainability remain the changes that clearly move the model.

WHY ATTENTION FAILED — what v23 does and does not establish. The attention injector is learned only during the unsupervised JEPA phase because the graph encoder remains frozen during supervised readout. The masked-node objective did not teach the hashed entity query which note spans matter for relation ranking. This experiment rejects an improvement from this training path; it does not show that entity-local note selection can never help. Training only the injector during readout, or querying with semantic entity text, is a new experiment and is not folded into this result.

The uniform control isolates the likely source of its small numerical lift. Its token-count-weighted span average reconstructs the original global mean at minimum cosine 0.999953 before the learned gate. Any difference is therefore more consistent with the separate gated injection path than with retained local note content.

DECISION: report v23 as a negative attention ablation. Do not claim uniform as a primary-endpoint improvement: +0.0098 is within the paired interval and below the with-note detection scale established by v20. The simplest supported note path remains the global mean; uniform is a numerical lead worth a targeted follow-up, not a new confirmed default.

THE MECHANISM: the note lift NARROWS under patch masking — exactly what v18 predicted. v18 argued patch masking recovers part of what the note was providing: a structure-only model has nothing but topology, so a harder pretext task forces it to learn the long-range regularities the note would otherwise hand it. That predicts the note should be worth less once patch masking is on, and it is: paired over 10 seeds the note lift falls from +0.1810 ±0.0135 at random masking (v21's configuration) to +0.1129 ±0.0496 under patch masking, both at 10/10.

Patch masking recovers ~0.068 MRR of what the note supplies — for free. No text, no 768 extra input dimensions, no Clinical-ModernBERT dependency at inference. For a deployment that cannot rely on note availability, that is the finding.

Caveats specific to this arm: (1) Not a strict replication of v18, which measured patch-vs-random at the DistMult head (+0.0237, 1 seed). This is the MLP head: direction replicates and is now significant, magnitudes are not like-for-like. (2) Patch masking without the note is unstable — sd 0.0453 against random's 0.0064, a 7× inflation, worse than the 1.6× seen with the note. The effect survives it, but this is not a tight arm. (3) Changing the pretext task changes phase 1, so the encoders genuinely differ.
Reading the with-note half — why its flat mean is a shape, not just a null:
• Eight of ten seeds land above 0.478 (every one beating v21's mean) while two — seeds 51 and 49 — drop to 0.4162 and 0.4448. Patch masking is a larger gain most of the time and an occasional substantial loss; that long left tail produces both the flat mean and the inflated spread (MRR sd 0.0167 → 0.0263, 1.6×; Hits@1 0.0171 → 0.0326, 1.9×, while Hits@10's is unchanged at 0.99×).
• H@10 moves, H@1 does not. A harder pretext task rescues targets that would otherwise fall out of the ranking entirely, without sharpening the fine discrimination that decides rank 1 from rank 2.
• v18 independently replicates the H@10 effect at a DIFFERENT readout head, four versions earlier: +0.0184 (DistMult, 1 seed) vs +0.0185 (MLP, 10 paired seeds) — agreement to 0.0001. Meanwhile their MRR deltas do not agree in sign (−0.0107 vs +0.0066). The metric that replicates is the metric that survives correction; the metrics that do not replicate are the ones neither report can distinguish from zero.
• Batch-mask agrees with LOO in both halves (n_mrr=3,000 — NOT comparable with LOO in absolute terms). With the note none of its three v22−v21 differences separates from zero; without it all three do. Against v20, the full stack is significant at 10/10 on every metric in both protocols — but that gain is v21's readout head, not the mask: +0.0596 LOO MRR total, of which the head supplied +0.0530.

Honest caveats: (1) v22 changes phase 1, so unlike v21-vs-v20 the two encoders are genuinely different models — the pairing controls the split and the seed, not the encoder. (2) H@10 is near saturation at 0.90, so +0.019 is better stated as a ~19% reduction in the miss rate, and it is the least discriminating of the four metrics. (3) JEPA_PATCHES=8 / NODE_MASK=0.4 throughout — patch count was never swept.

● v18 · 260804 — BFS-balanced patch masking: right idea, one seed per cell
The first experiment on the pretext task. One new module (src/fawkes/patches.py), one branch in jepa_step, two config fields. The default stayed v17's behaviour, so v18 is opt-in and every earlier gate still runs.

The problem with the v16/v17 mask. Phase 1 masked nodes i.i.d. Bernoulli at node_mask=0.4. Masking independently means a masked node's immediate neighbours are almost always still visible — a node of degree 5 keeps ~3 visible neighbours — so the pretext task is solvable by local interpolation: read the surviving neighbours, average, done. A task solvable one hop out never forces the encoder to learn anything longer-range, which is exactly the capability edge recovery needs.

What v18 does instead. Partition each graph into JEPA_PATCHES=8 balanced, connected-ish patches by multi-source BFS (farthest-point seeding: highest degree first, then greedily maximising distance from existing seeds), then mask whole patches until the node_mask fraction is reached. Targets become coherent regions, so a masked node's neighbours are usually masked too. Keeping the last-drawn patch as context makes the ≥1-target/≥1-context guarantee hold by construction rather than by retrying. Copied near-verbatim from clinical_jepa/graph/patches.py rather than imported, because tests/test_import_boundaries.py forbids the two lineages from importing each other.

▶ In Plain English — Read This First

What the model does. Each hospital admission becomes a graph: diagnoses, medications, procedures, cultures and services as dots, with typed connections between them. Some connections come free from the hospital tables; the interesting ones — this drug manages that diagnosis, this problem complicated that one — were drafted by an LLM. The model's job is to put back a connection we deliberately hid. That skill is what "refining the graph" means, and the score is LOO MRR: hide exactly one edge, show the model everything else, see how highly it ranks the right answer.

How it was supposed to work. Two phases. Phase 1 is the JEPA world model: hide chunks of the graph and make the model predict the meaning of what's missing, with no labels. Phase 2 freezes that and trains a small head to score edges. The claim is that phase 1 is where the intelligence comes from.

What seven versions of careful measurement actually found:

  • v23 tested whether the note should be local to each entity. Thirty matched runs compared the old global note mean, uniform 32-token spans and entity-conditioned attention. Attention did not improve the main score; it was 0.010 below the mean and 0.020 below uniform. Uniform was numerically best at 0.478, but its +0.010 primary-endpoint interval crossed zero. The honest result is negative: this unsupervised training path did not learn useful note attention.
  • v20 built the ruler first. Retraining the exact same configuration ten times, changing nothing but the random seed, moves the score by ±0.019. Any claimed improvement smaller than about 0.024 is indistinguishable from luck. The published number survived this check — the ten-run average landed 0.0012 away from it.
  • v21 found the actual win, and it was sitting in the code the whole time. Swapping the edge-scoring head from DistMult to a small MLP — a switch that had existed since the beginning and had never been flipped — raised the score from 0.417 to 0.471. It won at all ten seeds, and the two sets of results don't even overlap. All four of the relations the paper is about improved.
  • v19 asked the uncomfortable question. How much of the result comes from the JEPA pretraining? Answer: none that can be measured. A frozen, completely untrained encoder scores the same. Meanwhile the same architecture trained normally, with no pretraining at all, beats the paper model by +0.311. The likely cause is the freezing itself: a large lookup table of entity embeddings never receives any training signal in phase 2.
  • v18 and v22 tried making the pretraining harder (hide whole neighbourhoods instead of scattered dots). On the headline metric: nothing, twice. But one narrower metric — whether the right answer lands in the top ten — improved by the same amount in both experiments, four versions and one architecture apart. That agreement is why it is reported as real while the rest is not.
  • v17 is why any of this was affordable. It split the 719-line original into modules and proved — against a frozen copy of the old code — that not a single number moved. Because it preserved every setting verbatim, four of the five experiments above needed zero or almost zero new code. v21's entire discovery was one environment variable that v17 had faithfully carried forward.

One honest correction to the story. The paper is named after the self-supervised pretraining, and on the evidence collected here, every change to that pretraining sits inside the noise. The two things that genuinely move the model are the readout head and whether the encoder is allowed to train. Before rewriting anything, one check is owed: v19's conclusion is conditional on the pretraining having trained successfully at all, and the diagnostic that would confirm it (emb_std, already logged) has still not been read.

In one line: report v23 as a negative entity-attention ablation; uniform spans are numerically best at LOO MRR 0.478 ± 0.020 but not a confirmed improvement, so retain the simplest supported note path and confront directly that neither the attention injector nor the with-note pretext changes have improved the primary endpoint.
