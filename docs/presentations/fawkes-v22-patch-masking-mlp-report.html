Clinical Graph-JEPA · Engineering Report No. 6

v22: patch masking buys one metric, and costs variance

Turning on BFS-balanced patch masking alongside v21's MLP readout leaves the headline metric where it was — LOO MRR +0.0066 ± 0.0274, ahead at 6 of 10 seeds — while raising Hits@10 by a robust +0.0185 and inflating MRR variance by 1.6×. The Hits@10 effect independently replicates v18's four-year-old single-seed result to within 0.0001. §9–§12 work backwards through the lineage so this document stands alone.

repo clinical-graph-jepa runs 10 new (+20 reused) eval LOO 8,283 · batch-mask 3,000 seeds 42–51, paired split 42, fixed lineage v16 → v22 date 2026-08-08

§1The verdict, first

The readout head was the whole story; the pretext task is not. v21's DistMult → MLP swap moved LOO MRR by +0.0530 with the distributions disjoint. Adding patch masking on top moves it a further +0.0066 ± 0.0274 — a spread four times the effect, ahead at 6 of 10 seeds, and comfortably inside the ±0.019 noise floor v20 established. On the paper's headline metric, patch masking does nothing.

One metric does move, and it survives correction for testing seven of them: Hits@10 +0.0185 ± 0.0111, ahead at 9 of 10 seeds, t = 5.29. Patch masking gets the true target into the top ten more often without getting it any nearer the top — Hits@1 moves by +0.0014, which is nothing at all.

One structural difference from v21 is worth stating. v21 changed only the readout, which is built in phase 2, so both arms ran an identical, identically seeded phase 1 and the comparison isolated the head against the same encoder. v22 changes the pretext task, so phase 1 differs and the encoders are genuinely different models. §4The Hits@10 effect, and why v18 corroborates it

Patch masking raises Hits@10 by +0.0185 while leaving Hits@1 at +0.0014. It moves the true target into the top ten more reliably without moving it any closer to the top. That is a coherent thing for a harder pretext task to do — v18's own argument was that masking whole BFS patches removes the local-interpolation shortcut and forces longer-range structure into the encoder — and structural knowledge plausibly rescues targets that would otherwise fall out of the ranking entirely, without sharpening the fine discrimination that decides rank 1 from rank 2.

That reading would be speculation on one experiment. It is worth more than that because the effect already replicated, at a different readout head, four versions ago.

Two independent experiments, four versions apart, on different readout heads, with different baselines and different statistical designs, both put patch masking's Hits@10 gain at +0.018. Meanwhile their MRR deltas point in opposite directions and their Hits@1 deltas differ by 0.027. The metric that replicates is the metric that survives correction; the metrics that do not replicate are the ones this report cannot distinguish from zero. Those two facts agreeing is the strongest evidence here that the Hits@10 effect is real rather than the one lucky test in seven.

How much weight this can carry

v18's cell is a single seed compared against the released checkpoint rather than a retrained random-mask baseline, so it is neither paired nor powered — v20 established that one retrain can sit 1.1 sd from its own mean. Its Hits@10 number landing within 0.0001 of v22's is corroboration, not confirmation, and with one observation some of that precision is luck. It is enough to say the effect is not head-specific; it is not enough to quote a pooled estimate. A retrained (patch, DistMult) arm at ten seeds would settle it and would simultaneously give v18's original question the paired, powered answer it never got.

§5Seven tests, one survivor — and what it costs

This experiment ran seven paired comparisons for v22 − v21: four leave-one-out metrics and three batch-mask ones. At α = .05 you expect roughly one false positive from seven tests, so a single significant result is exactly the pattern a null effect produces. That objection has to be answered before Hits@10 is reported as a finding.

§6What to report

Keep v21 — random masking with the MLP readout — as the reported configuration. It matches v22 on MRR, Hits@1 and Hits@3 while being 1.6× tighter on the headline metric, and it needs one environment variable rather than two. Reporting a configuration that is no better on the metric you lead with, and noisier, is not a trade the paper should make.

§7What this does not establish

  • The 2×2 is still not complete on equal footing. v18's (patch, DistMult) cell is one unpaired seed. The interaction — whether patch masking behaves differently at the two heads — has a point estimate of about +0.016 on MRR and no interval at all.
  • One patch configuration only. JEPA_PATCHES=8 and NODE_MASK=0.4 throughout. Patch count is exactly the kind of knob that could turn a null result into an effect, and it was not swept.
  • Hits@10 is nearly saturated. At 0.90 there is little headroom, so a +0.019 gain is a ~19% reduction in the miss rate — arguably the better way to state it, and also a reminder that the metric is the least discriminating of the four.
  • Still one split. All thirty runs hold out the same 400 admissions. The reviewer's question about the patient sample remains open, as does the 36.8% patient overlap v20 §6 recorded.
  • auc_nonobvious remains broken and unreported. The v21 report established that _classification_metrics scores non-patient positives against an unfiltered negative pool. Until that is fixed, the column is omitted here rather than shown with a caveat.
  • Neither pretext task has been tested unfrozen. v19 showed the frozen protocol costs ~0.31 MRR. Every masking comparison in this series, v18 and v22 included, compares two frozen representations — the regime v19 suggests is the wrong one.

Config.checkpoint_dict writes a hand-picked subset of fields. §9v21 — the readout head

v21 · readout head
No source changes. One environment variable, ten jobs, v20 reused as the baseline arm.

build_scorer has returned an MLP head for any non-DistMult DECODER since the original trainer — the config pin maps decoder to a real trainer.py global rather than to null as genuinely new fields are. Nobody had run it. Doing so raised LOO MRR from 0.4174 to 0.4705, paired +0.0530 ± 0.0181, ahead at 10/10 seeds, with the two arms' distributions disjoint: DistMult's best run (0.4438) falls below the MLP's worst (0.4493). All four of the paper's inferred relations improved at 10/10 seeds, from +0.030 on MANAGED_FOR to +0.122 on CONFIRMS.

v21 also found that the sole apparently-negative result — auc_nonobvious down 0.0713 at 10/10 seeds — was a measurement artefact. _classification_metrics filters the positives to non-patient-anchored edges but scores them against the unfiltered negative pool, which is 68% patient-anchored. Matching the populations flips the sign to +0.041. The bug predates v21 and inflates the released checkpoint's published auc_nonobvious of 0.800 by the same mechanism. It is still unfixed.

§10v20 — the seed variance study

v20 · split/seed separation
One config field, one line in the splitter, one regression test. No change to any published number.

A reviewer asked for mean ± std across seeds and splits. Before v20, _split_items keyed its permutation on cfg.seed — the same value seeding the model — so changing SEED resampled the patients and reinitialised the model at once. data_split_seed separated them, defaulting to 42 so the published split is unchanged.

Ten retrains gave 0.4174 ± 0.0190 with all four published metrics inside the 95% CI and AUC agreeing to 0.0002 — the published number was not a lucky draw. It established the ±0.019 noise floor and the 0.0238 minimum detectable difference that every comparison since has been read against, including this one. v20 also found that no retrain in the lineage had ever reproduced the published number as a point: every apparent reproduction re-evaluates the released checkpoint.

v20's open items, still open

Every run in v20, v21 and v22 uses DATA_SPLIT_SEED=42, so the split axis remains unmeasured and the reviewer's actual question — is it a lucky sample of patients — is unanswered. v20 §6 also recorded that the split is by admission, not by patient: 139 subjects appear in both train and test, and 36.8% of test admissions belong to a patient the model trained on.

§11v19, v18 and the v16/v17 foundation

v19 — the JEPA pretraining ablation

Six arms across three seeds asked how much of the result comes from JEPA pretraining rather than from having any graph encoder. A frozen randomly initialised encoder scored the same as the JEPA-pretrained one (−0.008, sign flipping across seeds), while the same architecture trained end-to-end without phase 1 beat both by +0.311 MRR. An encoder with message passing removed entirely still beat the paper model by +0.23. The proposed mechanism: under FREEZE_ENCODER=1 the 8,192 × 128 entity embedding table is only ever shaped by the pretext task, and in the random-frozen arm never trained at all. v19's outstanding diagnostic — pulling emb_std from the arm-A logs to rule out target-encoder collapse — is still outstanding, and neither v21 nor v22 bears on it.

v18 — BFS-balanced patch masking

v16's pretext task masks nodes i.i.d. Bernoulli at 0.4, which lets the model solve masking by interpolating from surviving neighbours. v18 partitioned each graph into 8 BFS-balanced patches and masked whole patches, so a masked node's neighbours are usually masked too. Results were no-note +0.0237 and with-note −0.0107, one seed per cell — both inside the ±0.019 v20 later measured. The with-note comparison also set a freshly trained patch model against the released checkpoint rather than a retrained baseline, making it unpaired as well as underpowered. v22 is the paired, ten-seed version of that experiment at the new readout head, and §4 shows its Hits@10 result standing up.

v17 and v16 — the refactor and the experiment underneath

paper_v16/trainer.py read ~30 os.environ values at import and computed a tensor shape from them, so variables had to be set before import, two configurations could not coexist in one process, and the trainer was untestable. v17 split it into fawkes/{config,data,model,steps,train,evaluate}.py with every global becoming a Config field, gated on equality: LOO to 1e-6, identical state_dict keys, and an AST check proving no module-scope environment reads remain. All three still pass under v22, and it is that verbatim-preservation requirement that carried DECODER forward for v21 to find.

v16 itself retired v15's per-admission NOTE node and localised the same 768-dimensional Clinical-ModernBERT vector onto the entity nodes the note grounds — by default the endpoints of any edge carrying prov_in_note, 28.6% of nodes on the shipped dataset. Two TransformerConv layers, 2,637,954 parameters, numeric_dim=774, 60 JEPA epochs then 40 frozen readout epochs. Those defaults are still what Config() describes.
