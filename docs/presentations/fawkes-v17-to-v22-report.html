▸ THE OBJECTIVE — and the one number that measures it
Objective. Show that a Graph-JEPA world model over per-admission clinical knowledge graphs can recover held-out clinical relationships — specifically the four LLM-inferred cross-links (MANAGED_FOR, INDICATES, COMPLICATED_BY, CONFIRMS) that carry the paper's claim.

The metric that IS the objective = leave-one-out (LOO) MRR. Mask exactly ONE edge, keep the entire rest of the graph as context, rank the true tail against same-type candidates in the same graph, filtered. No randomness — exactly reproducible given a model and a test set. It is what the paper reports and what every number in this log is measured on unless a row says batch-mask. LOO evaluates almost exclusively the four inferred relations: the filtered protocol drops patient-anchored backbone edges, because after removing the other true tails of (patient, relation) fewer than two candidates usually remain.

Current best on the objective (10 paired seeds, fixed split): v21 — random masking + MLP readout, LOO MRR 0.4705 ± 0.0167, up from the published v16 DistMult configuration's 0.4174 ± 0.0190. The uncomfortable finding underneath it (v19): the JEPA pretext task — the part the method is named after — sits inside the noise floor on every variation tried, while the readout head and encoder trainability are what move the model.

Four measurements, one mechanism:
• A ≈ B. A frozen randomly initialised encoder scores the same as the JEPA-pretrained one. The spread (0.0178) is more than twice the mean effect (−0.0076), and the sign flips across seeds. That is the whole measured effect of JEPA pretraining.
• C >> A by +0.311. The identical architecture trained end-to-end without phase 1. Its smallest single-seed value (+0.1325) is still seven times the magnitude of the B−A mean.
• D separates memorisation from reasoning. With message passing removed entirely, 0.6293 is what learned entity identity alone buys — DistMult over a table that has seen which entities plausibly attach to which relations across 3,200 admissions. Honest decomposition at seed 42: 0.6293 from entity memorisation, +0.1624 from message passing on top (arm C), the paper model at 0.3962 below both.
• E < C by 0.2475 — the JEPA initialisation is worse than random. A representation that were merely neutral would land on top of C, not a quarter of an MRR below it.

The proposed mechanism: under FREEZE_ENCODER=1 the 8,192 × 128 hashed-entity embedding table receives no gradient in phase 2. In arm A it is shaped only by the pretext task; in arm B it is never trained at all and stays at its random initialisation for the whole run. Arms C/Cp/D train it on edge recovery itself. The frozen protocol — adopted so phase 2 would test the representation rather than let the GNN co-adapt to the decoder — succeeds at that, and the cost of the guarantee turns out to be most of the achievable performance.

The one code change, and its direction matters: train_readout's best-VAL selection snapshotted only the scorer. Under FREEZE_ENCODER=1 that is complete; every arm that trains the encoder got a best-VAL scorer bolted onto a final-epoch encoder — a pair that existed at no epoch. The bug degraded exactly the arms the JEPA claim is measured against, so leaving it in would have flattered the paper. Fixed by deep-copying the encoder state at the selected epoch, guarded on not cfg.freeze_encoder, so arms A and B run byte-identical code to v18. Gate: test_readout_selection_rolls_back_the_encoder_too — best VAL 0.707434, returned pair re-evaluated at 0.705473 before the fix, 0.707434 after.

If it decayed toward zero during arm A's 60 pretraining epochs, "phase 1 adds nothing" is a training failure, not a property of JEPA — and A ≈ B together with E < C is exactly the signature collapse would produce. ● v18 · 260804 — BFS-balanced patch masking: right idea, one seed per cell
The first experiment on the pretext task. One new module (src/fawkes/patches.py), one branch in jepa_step, two config fields. The default stayed v17's behaviour, so v18 is opt-in and every earlier gate still runs.

The problem with the v16/v17 mask. Phase 1 masked nodes i.i.d. Bernoulli at node_mask=0.4. Masking independently means a masked node's immediate neighbours are almost always still visible — a node of degree 5 keeps ~3 visible neighbours — so the pretext task is solvable by local interpolation: read the surviving neighbours, average, done. A task solvable one hop out never forces the encoder to learn anything longer-range, which is exactly the capability edge recovery needs.

What v18 does instead. Partition each graph into JEPA_PATCHES=8 balanced, connected-ish patches by multi-source BFS (farthest-point seeding: highest degree first, then greedily maximising distance from existing seeds), then mask whole patches until the node_mask fraction is reached. Targets become coherent regions, so a masked node's neighbours are usually masked too. Keeping the last-drawn patch as context makes the ≥1-target/≥1-context guarantee hold by construction rather than by retrying. Copied near-verbatim from clinical_jepa/graph/patches.py rather than imported, because tests/test_import_boundaries.py forbids the two lineages from importing each other.

● v17 · 260804 — the refactor that had to move nothing (and made v18–v22 cheap)
An engineering release, not an experiment. No numerics, no defaults, no tensor operations, and no environment-variable names changed — and that claim is gated, not asserted.

What v16 was, and the four problems: paper_v16/trainer.py, 719 lines, plus a separate checkpoint-only evaluate.py. (1) It configured itself from the environment at import time — ~30 os.environ reads ran on import, and NUMERIC_DIM, a tensor shape, was computed at import. Variables had to be set before the import, two configurations could not coexist in one process, and the trainer was effectively untestable. (2) Everything shared one namespace — data loading, the model, both training phases, four evaluators and reporting interleaved; the standalone evaluate.py reached into trainer.USE_NOTE. (3) --help trained the model — main() took no arguments and ran unconditionally, so fawkes-train --help completed a full training run and, with PUSH=1 the default, uploaded the result to the Hub. (4) The tensor code was unreadable (b, nt, ei, tot_llm) with a hardcoded date-stamped checkpoint filename.

The config refactor — the substantive design change. Every import-time global became a field on a Config dataclass; the environment is read only on demand by Config.from_env(), with the same variable names, defaults, and parsing rules (including the "1"/"true"/"yes" truthiness rule). Why this is the load-bearing release for everything above it. It is precisely the verbatim-preservation requirement that carried DECODER forward for v21 to find. v18 became a two-field change; v19 needed no new configuration at all; v20 needed one field; v21 and v22 needed zero source changes. Six experiments were reachable from one environment surface because v17 built that surface and proved it moved nothing.

▸ CORRECTION (260809) — auc_nonobvious fixed and the whole fleet re-scored

● The defect, the one-line fix, and what it does to every number that used it
The defect (pre-v21, carried through three reports). _classification_metrics filtered the positives to non-PATIENT-anchored edges — the harder entity-to-entity subset the metric exists to isolate — but concatenated them against the whole negative pool, including the negatives drawn for patient-anchored positives. readout_step returns neg_scores[:, 0]: one negative per positive, same length, same order, so the mask indexes the negatives exactly as it indexes the positives. Leaving them unmasked scored 2,509 non-patient positives against all 7,766 negatives, 67.7% of which belong to patient-anchored edges. Those are the easy ones, so the metric reported a discrimination the model never made — and inflated the arms unequally.

▶ In Plain English — Read This First

What the model does. Each hospital admission becomes a graph: diagnoses, medications, procedures, cultures and services as dots, with typed connections between them. Some connections come free from the hospital tables; the interesting ones — this drug manages that diagnosis, this problem complicated that one — were drafted by an LLM. The model's job is to put back a connection we deliberately hid. That skill is what "refining the graph" means, and the score is LOO MRR: hide exactly one edge, show the model everything else, see how highly it ranks the right answer.

How it was supposed to work. Two phases. Phase 1 is the JEPA world model: hide chunks of the graph and make the model predict the meaning of what's missing, with no labels. Phase 2 freezes that and trains a small head to score edges. The claim is that phase 1 is where the intelligence comes from.

What six versions of careful measurement actually found:

  • v20 built the ruler first. Retraining the exact same configuration ten times, changing nothing but the random seed, moves the score by ±0.019. Any claimed improvement smaller than about 0.024 is indistinguishable from luck. The published number survived this check — the ten-run average landed 0.0012 away from it.
  • v21 found the actual win, and it was sitting in the code the whole time. Swapping the edge-scoring head from DistMult to a small MLP — a switch that had existed since the beginning and had never been flipped — raised the score from 0.417 to 0.471. It won at all ten seeds, and the two sets of results don't even overlap. All four of the relations the paper is about improved.
  • v19 asked the uncomfortable question. How much of the result comes from the JEPA pretraining? Answer: none that can be measured. A frozen, completely untrained encoder scores the same. Meanwhile the same architecture trained normally, with no pretraining at all, beats the paper model by +0.311. The likely cause is the freezing itself: a large lookup table of entity embeddings never receives any training signal in phase 2.
  • v18 and v22 tried making the pretraining harder (hide whole neighbourhoods instead of scattered dots). On the headline metric: nothing, twice. But one narrower metric — whether the right answer lands in the top ten — improved by the same amount in both experiments, four versions and one architecture apart. That agreement is why it is reported as real while the rest is not.
  • v17 is why any of this was affordable. It split the 719-line original into modules and proved — against a frozen copy of the old code — that not a single number moved. Because it preserved every setting verbatim, four of the five experiments above needed zero or almost zero new code. v21's entire discovery was one environment variable that v17 had faithfully carried forward.

One honest correction to the story. The paper is named after the self-supervised pretraining, and on the evidence collected here, every change to that pretraining sits inside the noise. The two things that genuinely move the model are the readout head and whether the encoder is allowed to train. Before rewriting anything, one check is owed: v19's conclusion is conditional on the pretraining having trained successfully at all, and the diagnostic that would confirm it (emb_std, already logged) has still not been read.

In one line: report v21 — random masking with the MLP readout, LOO MRR 0.471 ± 0.017 over ten seeds — note patch masking's Hits@10 gain as a secondary result, fix the one broken metric before anything ships, and confront directly that the pretext task the method is named after has not yet been shown to matter.
